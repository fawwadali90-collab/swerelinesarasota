Sewer problems are hard to describe in a form field, is that gurgling from one fixture or the whole house, did the backup start after the last storm, how old is the line, and a two-minute conversation answers what a form takes a day of back-and-forth to establish.

What the Call Leads To

The call itself is short. The inspection is the real first step: we run a camera through your lateral, show you the footage, explain what is wrong in plain language, and lay out the repair options your pipe's condition supports. You make the decision from there, with the evidence in front of you.

What Happens After You Call

Here is the sequence, exactly as it runs. First, we schedule the camera inspection and confirm the access point, usually an existing cleanout, or we identify where one needs to be established. Second, we run the camera and record the full lateral to the main, narrating what we see as we go.

Third, we show you the footage and explain the findings: where the damage is, how extensive it is, what material the pipe is, and whether it still holds its shape. Fourth, we recommend the repair method the evidence supports, jetting and root treatment, lining, bursting, or a targeted conventional repair, and explain why the alternatives do or do not fit. Fifth, you decide. There is no pressure built into the process and no obligation attached to the inspection.

When to Call Now Versus Soon

Call right away if sewage is backing up into the building, if you smell strong sewer gas indoors, or if multiple fixtures stopped draining at once. Those are active failures, and every flush or laundry load can make the inside-the-house part worse.

Call soon, this week, not this month, for the warning signs: a single drain that keeps slowing down, gurgling that comes and goes, a patch of yard that stays wet between rains, or roots that were cleared before and are back. Sarasota's June to September rainy season turns warning signs into failures fast, because saturated soil and a rising water table stress every weak joint in the line. The dry months are the best time to inspect and plan; the wet months are when neglected lines fail.

What We Will Ask You

To make the call efficient, it helps to have a few things in mind before you dial. What symptoms are you seeing, and when did they start? Do they affect one fixture or the whole building? Did they begin or worsen after heavy rain? Do you know roughly when your home was built, and do you know whether you are on county sewer or a septic tank?

None of this is required, we can work it out together on the call, but the answers sharpen the first visit. The era of the house tells us what pipe to expect, the rain timing tells us whether groundwater infiltration is in play, and the sewer-versus-septic answer determines whether a conversion conversation belongs on the table at all.
